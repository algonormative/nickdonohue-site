# Complexity as a Lens — Full Develop Session

## The Conversation (2026-03-10)

### Seed

Looking at Hopalong attractors (Barry Martin, 1986). Simple recursive formula:

```
x_{n+1} = y_n - sgn(x_n)√|bx_n - c|
y_{n+1} = a - x_n
```

Three parameters, start at origin, iterate. The visual complexity comes from plotting millions of points and watching density patterns emerge. Pixel density *is* the art. Different parameter combinations yield vastly different outcomes — from dense chaotic regions to periodic orbits.

Source: https://github.com/ratwolfzero/hopalong_python

### What grabbed me (verbatim)

> I think because it feels like exploration: we're essentially probing into a theoretically infinite expanse of possibilities, and some states have certain significance to us.

**Claude:** What do you mean by "significance to us"? Aesthetic, or something deeper?

> In this context, the goal is aesthetics, but in other cases, its useful. You, for instance, are a complex combination of mathematics that produces useful (and much valued!) output. I as well am ultimately a combination of my neurons, my life context and experience. Attractor, AI, Human, none of us are truly random but the result of a complex system. I wish to ultimately understand complexity.

**Claude:** When you say you want to "understand complexity" — what does that actually look like for you?

> Articulation is definitely a challenge, and I don't expect to understand it fully in my lifetime. So in real life its mostly a study, and a worldview I use to channel what I create, and want to create.

**Claude:** So complexity isn't a problem you're solving — it's a lens you look through.

### The progression

**Claude:** Who do you imagine reading this?

> The latter, I think I just want to chat about it rather than think about audience.

**Claude:** Can you give me a concrete example where the complexity lens shaped a decision you made?

> The news game was a big moment for me, because it got me thinking about human cultures, history, how we developed, the idea for making the news game led me to want to try to understand the "full algorithm" to human development, since I would kind of have to simulate human society if I wanted to make a game about the news. Thinking a lot about that helped channel things. Before that, I was into abstract mathematical, procedural and generative art (still am), I like the idea of figuring out the mathematical geometry of things and drawing it out instead of hand-drawing.

**Claude:** So there's a progression: pure math/geometry (beauty of systems in the abstract) → news game (human systems, same complexity impulse but messier). Going from "I can define this with an equation and render it" to "I'd need to simulate all of human society to make this game work" — that's the moment where complexity stops being elegant and starts being humbling.

> I think I'm drawn towards the underlying rules, though I only want to see patterns, specifics are harder for me, they require more effort to remember. I like the idea of my effort "scaling" well, but fall into the trap of endlessly thinking about the general process instead of making things specifically.
>
> For instance, since I've signed up for claude code I've been busy making software to help me with art and music, but I end up spending more time in here than actually creating the art. I believe this is because I spend more effort trying to scale my efforts to make the art rather than actually creating the art. This is why I haven't finished many things.

**Claude:** You're drawn to the parameter space — the meta-level, the system that generates the output — when the actual art/music/thing requires you to pick specific parameters and commit. You could explore parameter space forever, or you could pick a set of values and render the image. That tension — is that the honest core of this? Not "complexity is beautiful" (true but safe), but "I'm drawn to complexity and it's both the most interesting thing about me and the thing that keeps me from finishing."

**Claude:** When you *have* finished something — what was different?

> 1. it felt right, 2. some finishable product was ready within the scope of me being able to stay interested in it.

### The landing

> Perhaps a better way to describe this is that by analogy I'm always trying to find beautiful (convergent maybe?) shapes from these equations, but never stop to share the beauty of individual attractor states. By stopping to smell the flowers so to speak, we give ourselves the ability to truly live.

**Claude:** That's the ending of the post. The whole piece builds up the complexity worldview — attractors, AI, human minds, the infinite parameter space — and then lands on: *but if you never stop to render one image, you never actually experience the beauty you're chasing.* Exploring is seductive. Committing is where the living happens.

## Claude's Analysis

### Emerging Thesis

Complexity isn't a problem to solve — it's a lens to look through. The Hopalong attractor is a perfect concrete illustration: you could explore parameter space forever, or you could pick values and render the image. The author sees this tension in himself — drawn to the rules behind the rules, which is both a superpower (makes unexpected connections) and a trap (optimizes process over output). The irony: by always searching for the next beautiful attractor state, you never stop to appreciate the one in front of you.

### Arc

1. Here's a Hopalong attractor — simple formula, infinite space, some points are beautiful
2. I see this pattern everywhere — AI, humans, all emergent from complex systems
3. I'm drawn to the meta-level, the rules behind the rules, and that's kept me from finishing things
4. The progression: abstract generative art → news game → "I'd need to simulate human society" → humbled by complexity
5. The irony: by always searching for the next beautiful attractor state, I never stop to appreciate the one in front of me
6. Stopping to smell the flowers is the whole point
